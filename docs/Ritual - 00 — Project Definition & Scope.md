# **1\. Executive Summary**

## **1.1 Project Overview**

Ritual is a cinematic, browser-based interactive 3D experience that transforms the familiar process of making espresso into a tactile, visually immersive digital experience.

At its center is a believable automatic bean-to-cup espresso coffee machine, presented as a carefully art-directed physical object.

Visitors do not merely watch an animation. They operate the machine.

They discover its mechanical components, open the bean hopper, add coffee beans, position a cup, activate the machine, initiate the coffee-making process, observe the grinding and brewing sequence, and finally control the dispensing of the prepared coffee.

The experience unfolds through direct physical interaction, responsive animation, lighting, sound, environmental detail, and observable consequences.

Unlike a conventional website, Ritual contains no visible navigation, text instructions, menus, or external control interface.

The object itself communicates what can be done.

Ritual was conceived for the RCA Art & Design Club exhibition, where the work is intended to demonstrate the intersection of digital art, 3D design, interaction design, and creative engineering.

The project serves two primary purposes:

1. **Exhibition:** Produce an engaging and memorable interactive installation that demonstrates the club's creative and technical capabilities.  
2. **Creative exploration:** Investigate how cinematic direction, real-time rendering, physical interaction, and visual storytelling can elevate an ordinary real-world process into an immersive digital experience.

Ritual is not a traditional commercial website, a marketing presentation, or an engineering-accurate coffee-machine simulator.

It is an interactive digital product film in which the visitor participates directly in the action.

## **1.2 Core Creative Statement**

> Take an ordinary, recognizable physical object and transform the experience of operating it into a cinematic interaction without sacrificing its believability.

The subject is not merely a coffee machine.

The subject is the process.

The visitor's actions initiate and influence the sequence, while the machine performs the internal mechanical work.

Every meaningful interaction should produce a visible or audible consequence.

The experience should communicate through its own physical behavior rather than through an explanatory interface.

**The machine is the interface. The process is the narrative. The visitor completes the ritual.**

# **2\. Project Background & Motivation**

## **2.1 Exhibition Context**

Ritual originated from the need to create an interactive exhibition for the RCA Art & Design Club.

The exhibition is intended to introduce new students to the club and communicate what its members can create.

Instead of presenting a slideshow, poster, or collection of static work, Ritual demonstrates creative capability through direct participation.

The visitor should not need someone standing beside the screen explaining the interaction.

A person approaching the exhibit should recognize a familiar machine, become curious about its components, discover that it can be operated, and gradually understand its behavior through experimentation.

The experience should encourage visitors to show one another what they have discovered.

The ideal visitor reaction is not simply admiration of the rendering quality.

It is the realization that the machine responds to them and that their actions have meaningful consequences.

## **2.2 Why an Espresso Machine?**

The coffee machine was selected because it combines three important qualities:

**Familiarity**

Most visitors can recognize a coffee machine or infer its basic purpose, even if they have never operated this exact type.

**Sequential operation**

The machine naturally supports a recognizable process involving preparation, activation, internal mechanical activity, waiting, and dispensing.

**Physical interaction opportunities**

The machine provides several meaningful interaction points:

* A hopper that can be opened.  
* Coffee beans that can be transferred into the machine.  
* A movable cup.  
* Physical controls.  
* Mechanical preparation.  
* A visible liquid output.

These components create an experience with an intuitive beginning, middle, and conclusion.

The machine does not need to be fictional, unusually complicated, or technologically futuristic to become interesting.

The artistic interest comes from how its ordinary operation is presented.

## **2.3 Why Ritual?**

Making coffee is often a repeated routine.

Ritual transforms that routine into a sequence of deliberate gestures, mechanical responses, and moments of anticipation.

The act of opening the hopper becomes an invitation to explore.

Adding beans establishes preparation.

Pressing Power wakes the machine.

Grinding creates mechanical tension.

The transition into brewing changes the rhythm.

The moment before pouring introduces anticipation.

The flowing coffee provides the visual payoff.

The name Ritual expresses this choreography of repeated physical actions.

The tagline, **The Choreography of Espresso**, describes the project's artistic emphasis on movement, timing, interaction, and process.

# **3\. Vision, Mission & Objectives**

## **3.1 Vision**

Create a believable digital physical object whose operation feels tactile, responsive, and cinematic.

The final experience should blur the distinction between interacting with a real product and participating in a carefully directed product film.

It should remain visually compelling without becoming decorative or unnecessarily complicated.

## **3.2 Mission**

Combine 3D product design, real-time rendering, direct manipulation, motion design, camera choreography, responsive audio, and coherent state management into one polished browser experience.

Every technical decision should support at least one of the following:

* Physical believability.  
* Interaction quality.  
* Visual clarity.  
* Emotional pacing.  
* Exhibition reliability.

Technology should remain subordinate to the experience.

## **3.3 Primary Objectives**

### **Objective 01 — Demonstrate Creative Engineering**

Show how artistic decisions and technical systems can produce a coherent interactive experience.

The project should demonstrate more than the ability to render a 3D model.

It should demonstrate how visual systems, interaction architecture, timing, and technical implementation work together.

### **Objective 02 — Deliver Intuitive Physical Interaction**

Visitors should understand the machine by interacting with its recognizable components.

The interface must be understandable through geometry, placement, movement, lighting, sound, and consequences rather than instructional text.

### **Objective 03 — Establish a Cinematic Product Experience**

Present the machine with carefully directed composition, lighting, materials, motion, and camera behavior.

The final result should feel closer to premium product cinematography than a typical interactive 3D demo.

### **Objective 04 — Create Meaningful Cause and Effect**

Actions should produce logically connected consequences.

Adding beans changes the machine's preparation condition.

Pressing Make initiates a mechanical process.

Cup position affects where coffee falls.

Stopping Pour interrupts dispensing.

Interactions must affect the experience rather than trigger unrelated visual effects.

### **Objective 05 — Preserve User Agency**

The visitor controls preparation, activation, and dispensing.

The machine controls its internal operation.

Cinematic choreography may guide attention, but it must not replace or undermine the visitor's meaningful decisions.

### **Objective 06 — Deliver Exhibition-Ready Reliability**

The experience must support repeated visitors, a complete interaction cycle, and predictable reset behavior.

It should recover gracefully from invalid actions and interrupted sessions.

### **Objective 07 — Maintain a Clear Project Source of Truth**

The project's approved decisions should be documented independently from any particular implementation.

The experience must be reproducible and understandable without relying on the original developer's memory.

# **5\. Fundamental Experience Principles**

## **5.1 The Object Is the Interface**

This is a non-negotiable creative rule.

The experience must not rely on conventional screen-based interface elements.

The active scene should contain no:

* Visible title or heading.  
* Navigation.  
* Instructional text.  
* Tooltips.  
* External action buttons.  
* Menus.  
* Progress bars.  
* Tutorials.  
* Textual state indicators.  
* Artificial gameplay interface.

The machine's actual components are the controls.

The camera is a way of inspecting the physical object, not a means of navigating a website.

## **5.2 Familiarity Over Fiction**

The machine must remain physically plausible.

The experience can exaggerate animation, lighting, camera movement, atmosphere, and visual emphasis, but should not transform the machine into a futuristic or fictional artifact.

The intended formula is:

**Real physical object \+ exceptional art direction.**

## **5.3 Interaction Before Spectacle**

The visitor should not simply trigger a series of predetermined animations.

The major actions must originate from visitor decisions.

The machine's internal sequences may be automated because they represent mechanical processes rather than visitor-controlled tasks.

Spectacle emerges from the interaction, not the other way around.

## **5.4 Every Action Has a Consequence**

The experience should establish consistent relationships between action and feedback.

**Action → Response → Consequence**

This is the foundation of Ritual's interaction design.

## **5.5 Complexity Must Remain Invisible**

The underlying systems may use sophisticated animation, state coordination, procedural rendering, or other advanced techniques.

The visitor should not experience that complexity as operational difficulty.

The interaction should feel natural, smooth, and straightforward.

## **5.6 One Object, One Process, One Experience**

Ritual should not expand into a collection of products, recipes, or unrelated interactions.

Its identity depends on concentrating artistic and engineering effort into one coherent process.

# **6\. Core Experience Definition**

## **6.1 High-Level Journey**

The experience follows this overall progression:

**Discover → Prepare → Position → Activate → Process → Wait → Dispense → Control → Complete → Reset**

The intended showcase sequence is preparation-first.

The visitor prepares the physical machine before powering it on.

This preserves the original experience concept and reinforces the idea that the user must supply the machine with what it needs.

## **6.2 Stage 01 — Discovery**

The visitor enters the scene.

The machine is inactive.

The setup is visible in a carefully composed three-quarter view.

The hopper, controls, beans, cup, and outlet are visually identifiable.

No instructions are displayed.

The visitor may inspect the machine using controlled camera movement.

**Experience goal:** Curiosity and recognition.

## **6.3 Stage 02 — Open the Hopper**

The visitor discovers that the hopper lid can be opened.

Interacting with the lid produces a physically motivated opening motion accompanied by a subtle mechanical sound.

The open lid reveals the bean compartment.

**Experience goal:** Discovery of a meaningful physical interaction.

## **6.4 Stage 03 — Add Coffee Beans**

The visitor picks up or manipulates the coffee beans/container and adds the beans to the open hopper.

The beans should visibly transfer into the machine, fall, and settle.

The interaction should be more meaningful than simply clicking an object and hiding it.

The implementation may use a simplified controlled animation, but the visual result must communicate an intentional ingredient transfer.

**Experience goal:** Preparation and agency.

## **6.5 Stage 04 — Close the Hopper**

The visitor closes the hopper.

The lid returns to its resting position with a believable mechanical response.

The machine is now supplied with its primary ingredient.

**Experience goal:** Completion of the ingredient preparation step.

## **6.6 Stage 05 — Position the Cup**

The visitor moves the cup beneath the coffee outlet.

The cup is a genuine movable object rather than a fixed decorative element.

Its position matters during dispensing.

The required placement should feel natural and forgiving enough for exhibition use.

**Experience goal:** Understanding the relationship between machine output and receiving vessel.

## **6.7 Stage 06 — Power On**

The visitor presses the physical Power button.

The machine changes from dormant to active.

Its activation is communicated through an appropriate combination of physical button response, mechanical sound, and a subtle machine-integrated indicator.

**Experience goal:** Transformation from inactive object to functioning machine.

## **6.8 Stage 07 — Make Coffee**

The visitor presses the physical Make button.

The machine begins preparing coffee.

It must not immediately dispense the liquid.

The preparation initiates an internally controlled sequence.

**Experience goal:** Anticipation.

## **6.9 Stage 08 — Grinding**

The machine enters the grinding stage.

The process is communicated through synchronized mechanical sound, subtle vibration, and believable visual activity.

The internal grinder does not require mechanically accurate simulation.

The machine must simply feel as though it is performing real work.

**Experience goal:** Mechanical tension and engagement.

## **6.10 Stage 09 — Mechanical Pause**

Grinding concludes.

A short period of relative quiet separates grinding from brewing.

This pause is an intentional part of the experience's temporal structure.

It prevents the process from feeling like a continuous stream of unrelated effects.

**Experience goal:** Contrast and anticipation.

## **6.11 Stage 10 — Brewing**

The machine begins brewing.

Sound, subtle movement, and machine behavior suggest pressure, pumping, heat, and internal preparation.

The experience should communicate the process without exposing an unnecessary, mechanically accurate internal assembly.

**Experience goal:** Convincing transformation.

## **6.12 Stage 11 — Ready to Pour**

Brewing completes.

The machine enters a waiting condition.

The prepared coffee is available, but dispensing does not begin automatically.

A subtle physical indicator or machine response may communicate readiness.

No textual status message appears.

**Experience goal:** Anticipation and restored user control.

## **6.13 Stage 12 — Pouring**

The visitor presses the physical Pour button.

Coffee begins flowing from the outlet.

The liquid must appear visually connected to the machine and obey a believable downward path.

When a cup is correctly positioned, it gradually fills.

This is one of the primary cinematic moments of the experience.

**Experience goal:** Visual payoff.

## **6.14 Stage 13 — User-Controlled Dispensing**

The visitor can press Pour again to stop the flow.

The result must respond promptly and visibly.

The visitor may also experiment with the cup's position where the interaction system permits it.

Cup misalignment can cause coffee to miss the cup.

Continued pouring may produce an overflow consequence.

**Experience goal:** Control and physical cause and effect.

## **6.15 Stage 14 — Completion**

The dispensing operation concludes.

The scene reflects the outcome of the visitor's actions.

The machine settles into an appropriate completed condition.

The experience should feel resolved without relying on a success screen or external UI.

**Experience goal:** Satisfaction.

## **6.16 Stage 15 — Reset & Replay**

The experience must be capable of returning to its original condition.

Reset restores the machine, cup, hopper, beans, coffee effects, camera, active sound, and logical state as appropriate.

The precise visitor-facing and exhibition-operator reset mechanisms are to be defined in Document 03\.

**Experience goal:** Reliable repetition.

## **6.17 Sequence Flexibility**

The previous sequence describes the intended showcase journey.

It does not mean that every preparatory action must be artificially locked behind the previous one.

Physical actions may be possible in different orders when that behavior remains coherent.

For example, a visitor may explore the cup before opening the hopper.

However, machine processing must respect its prerequisites.

The exact action guards, interruptions, and state transitions belong in Document 03\.

# **7\. Primary Interactions & Physical Controls**

## **7.1 Interactive Object Inventory**

| Object | Required behavior |
| ----- | ----- |
| Hopper lid | Opens and closes physically |
| Coffee beans/container | Can be manipulated to transfer beans into the hopper |
| Coffee cup | Can be moved and positioned relative to the outlet |
| Power button | Activates the machine |
| Make button | Starts the preparation process |
| Pour button | Starts and stops dispensing |
| Camera | Supports constrained exploration of the scene |

The coffee stream, cup-fill surface, steam, indicator lights, and environmental effects are reactive elements rather than separate primary controls.

## **7.2 Button Responsibilities**

### **Power**

Responsible exclusively for machine activation and the applicable power state.

The Power control must not also serve as Make or Pour.

### **Make**

Responsible for initiating the grinding and brewing sequence when machine prerequisites are satisfied.

Repeated activation must not incorrectly duplicate an ongoing preparation sequence.

### **Pour**

Responsible for controlling the coffee output after preparation is complete.

First valid press: start dispensing.

Second valid press: stop dispensing.

The control must have a clear, consistent physical role.

## **7.3 Physical Feedback**

Every successful physical interaction should have feedback appropriate to the material and mechanism.

Examples:

* A button depresses and returns.  
* A hopper lid moves on its hinge.  
* Beans visibly fall.  
* A cup follows the visitor's movement.  
* A machine activates.  
* Liquid begins and ends flowing.

Feedback should be immediate enough to communicate that the visitor's action registered.

## **7.4 Invalid Actions**

An invalid action must not corrupt the machine state.

Examples include attempting to begin brewing without required preparation or attempting to pour before the machine has prepared coffee.

The preferred behavior should remain physically coherent and avoid unnecessary error messages.

Detailed feedback and recovery rules will be specified separately.

## **7.5 User Control vs Machine Control**

The visitor controls:

* Exploration.  
* Hopper operation.  
* Bean preparation.  
* Cup placement.  
* Power activation.  
* Beginning the coffee-making sequence.  
* Beginning and stopping dispensing.

The machine controls:

* Grinding.  
* The intentional transition pause.  
* Brewing.  
* Internal preparation timing.  
* Readiness after preparation.

This separation preserves both user agency and cinematic timing.

# **8\. Creative Direction**

## **8.1 Core Visual Language**

**Warm Minimalism \+ Premium Real Product Design**

The machine should feel:

* Believable.  
* Tactile.  
* Contemporary.  
* Premium.  
* Carefully engineered.  
* Visually restrained.  
* Cinematic.

It should not feel fictional, excessively futuristic, game-like, or unnecessarily industrial.

## **8.2 Product Design**

The machine is a compact consumer appliance.

Its physical dimensions should remain approximately consistent with the original concept, with the product modeled around:

* Width: approximately 35 cm.  
* Depth: approximately 40 cm.  
* Height: approximately 40 cm.

These are intended product dimensions; final geometry and scene units must be validated when establishing the production asset contract.

## **8.3 Material Language**

The primary material direction consists of:

**Semi-matte charcoal:** Dominant machine housing.

**Brushed satin metal:** Selected functional panels and trim.

**Transparent hopper material:** Reveals the ingredient and creates material contrast.

**White ceramic:** Cup material that contrasts with the darker machine.

**Coffee and coffee beans:** Warm, natural dark-brown material accents.

Materials should be physically believable, with controlled roughness, reflections, transparency, and highlights.

## **8.4 Composition**

The coffee machine remains the central visual subject.

The input and output relationships should be immediately understandable:

**Beans → Hopper → Machine → Outlet → Cup**

The surrounding scene exists to support this hierarchy.

The design should not be crowded with unnecessary secondary objects.

## **8.5 Environment**

The environment should resemble a restrained product-photography stage with enough physical context to ground the machine.

It should include:

* A believable supporting surface.  
* A subtle background or backdrop.  
* Soft shadows.  
* Appropriate reflections.  
* Controlled atmospheric depth.  
* Lighting that reveals the machine's materials.

It must not become a fully furnished kitchen.

The exact backdrop treatment and light placement will be refined in Document 04\.

## **8.6 Lighting**

Lighting should be soft, dimensional, and deliberate.

A broad key light establishes the machine's form.

Fill preserves important material details.

Restrained edge lighting provides separation.

Machine-integrated lights may participate in the activation and preparation states.

The lighting must reveal the dark product materials without flattening their appearance.

## **8.7 Camera**

The default composition should use a controlled three-quarter perspective.

The user may inspect the machine through a restricted orbit.

The original reference direction calls for approximately ±150–170° of horizontal inspection, with restrained vertical movement and zoom.

The exact bounds will be finalized through visual testing.

The camera may gently guide attention during important mechanical moments, but should avoid aggressive movement and loss of user control.

## **8.8 Animation**

Animation must communicate material and mechanical qualities.

Buttons should feel responsive.

The hopper lid should imply weight and hinge resistance.

The machine should have subtle mechanical movement during operation.

The coffee stream should appear organic rather than rigid.

All transitions should be coordinated as part of one motion system.

## **8.9 Sound**

The sound design should focus on believable machine and object interactions:

* Lid movement.  
* Bean handling.  
* Cup placement.  
* Button actuation.  
* Machine startup.  
* Grinding.  
* Pumping.  
* Brewing.  
* Pouring.  
* Steam.

Silence and transitions between sounds are as important as the sounds themselves.

Audio should contribute to pacing, physicality, and anticipation.

# **11\. Interaction State Boundaries**

## **11.1 State Model Purpose**

The machine must maintain a consistent understanding of its own condition.

The implementation should not depend on unrelated component animations independently deciding the machine's operating state.

## **11.2 Conceptual States**

The experience requires conceptual conditions corresponding to:

* Machine off.  
* Hopper open/closed.  
* Beans absent/present.  
* Cup position and alignment.  
* Machine powered on.  
* Ready for preparation.  
* Grinding.  
* Transition pause.  
* Brewing.  
* Ready to pour.  
* Actively pouring.  
* Pour stopped/completed.  
* Resetting or restoring the initial state.

These are conceptual categories, not prescribed TypeScript enum values.

Preparatory object conditions may coexist with power conditions.

For example, the hopper may be opened and beans added while the machine remains powered off.

This is essential to the approved preparation-first direction.

## **11.3 Primary Guards**

The system must enforce logical prerequisites.

The machine must not begin grinding until its necessary preparation conditions are satisfied.

It must not begin pouring until brewing is complete.

An active preparation process must not be duplicated through repeated Make interactions.

Reset must safely terminate pending behavior.

## **11.4 Timing Ownership**

The user controls when preparation and dispensing begin.

The machine controls the timing of grinding and brewing.

Timing must belong to a coherent process system so that animation, audio, state, and camera behavior remain synchronized.

## **11.5 Interrupted Interaction**

Power interruption, mid-process input, cup movement, rapid repeated clicks, and reset during animation must be handled coherently.

The detailed policies will be resolved in Document 03\.

No particular previous implementation behavior is assumed.

# **12\. Non-Functional Requirements**

## **12.1 Visual Fidelity**

The experience should maintain convincing material response, composition, shadows, lighting, and physical scale.

Important machine features must remain legible.

Effects should not obscure the object or introduce obvious visual artifacts.

## **12.2 Interaction Responsiveness**

Inputs should produce timely feedback.

Longer delays are acceptable only when they represent purposeful machine activity.

Interactions must not feel broken because a mechanical sequence is still running.

## **12.3 Performance**

The application should be optimized for its target exhibition hardware.

A stable 60 FPS presentation is the preferred goal where practical.

Final acceptance thresholds must be established after the exact hardware is known.

Avoid unnecessary draw calls, excessive real-time lights, heavy postprocessing, redundant updates, and uncontrolled GPU allocations.

## **12.4 Reliability**

The experience should withstand repeated visitor interactions.

State and animation must remain consistent.

Reset should terminate active sounds, delayed operations, and animations safely.

The application must not require a page refresh after an ordinary completed interaction.

## **12.5 Loading & Recovery**

The 3D experience must not expose a partially initialized scene.

Loading and asset failure behavior must be deliberate and reliable.

Any technical failure presentation should remain distinct from the normal no-UI experience and should not become part of its artistic interface.

## **12.6 Audio Reliability**

Browser audio must initialize according to browser interaction policies.

Audio must be synchronized with machine events.

Sound loops must stop when their corresponding mechanical stages end or are interrupted.

## **12.7 Accessibility & Device Considerations**

The experience should avoid making essential interaction feedback exclusively audible.

Pointer interactions should remain predictable and reasonably forgiving.

Alternative input or accessibility accommodations should be considered without compromising the artistic purpose of the scene.

Any reduced-motion requirements and touch-support commitments must be explicitly determined before release.

## **12.8 Maintainability**

The implementation should separate responsibilities among interaction, rendering, state, animation, camera, audio, and effects systems.

Important constants should be documented and centralized where appropriate.

The architecture should support iteration without requiring the entire scene to be rewritten.

# **22\. Risks & Mitigations**

| Risk | Mitigation |
| ----- | ----- |
| Interaction feels like clicking a static 3D model | Design meaningful object manipulation and feedback |
| The visitor cannot discover the process | Strengthen physical affordances and visual hierarchy |
| Camera movement competes with interaction | Limit automation and preserve visitor control |
| Model does not support animation | Validate nodes, pivots, and geometry before integration |
| Liquid looks artificial | Use carefully art-directed geometry, lighting, and motion |
| Machine operation feels disconnected | Synchronize state, animation, and sound |
| State transitions become inconsistent | Define and test a coherent state model |
| Too many effects reduce performance | Prioritize visual impact per computational cost |
| Production scope expands excessively | Enforce must/should/nice-to-have boundaries |
| Audio feels synthetic or distracting | Review sound quality within the complete sequence |
| Reset is incomplete | Treat recovery as a core feature |
| Documentation and implementation diverge | Review changes against approved specifications |
| Exhibition device cannot run the experience | Test on actual target hardware before release |

# **23\. Final Project Statement**

Ritual is an exploration of how thoughtful creative engineering can transform an ordinary physical action into a compelling interactive experience.

It is not a showcase of maximum technological complexity.

It is a showcase of restraint, coordination, physicality, and artistic intention.

A carefully modeled machine, a handful of meaningful interactions, believable materials, considered camera movements, expressive mechanical sound, and a well-paced coffee-making sequence should combine into a single unified experience.

The visitor should not feel that they are navigating software.

They should feel that they are operating something.

The visual sophistication should invite attention.

The physical interactions should reward curiosity.

The mechanical sequence should create anticipation.

The pour should provide the payoff.

The completion should feel satisfying.

**The machine is the interface.**

**The process is the narrative.**

**The visitor completes the ritual.**
